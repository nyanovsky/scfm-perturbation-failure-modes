Gene embeddings beat co-expression at predicting knockdown response, and protein sequence alone supplies them

A gene-pair feature taken from a single-cell foundation model predicts the perturbation-specific part of a CRISPRi knockdown response better than statistical co-expression computed from the same control cells. On held-out perturbations in HepG2 the best model feature reaches 0.2719 (0.2394 to 0.3814) (scGPT embedding) against 0.04879 (0.02346 to 0.09292) for co-expression, and in Jurkat 0.2371 (0.1831 to 0.2938) (TranscriptFormer embedding + response) against 0.04462 (0.0302 to 0.0617). Paired on the same held-out perturbations the difference is 0.1846 (0.1501 to 0.2408) and 0.1371 (0.09442 to 0.1819). This is the pre-registered scoring rule, and every interval on this page resamples whole response groups rather than individual perturbations, because correlated perturbations are held out together and are not independent draws.

The feature doing that work is the model's static gene-identity table, and two of the four tables were not learned from cells. TranscriptFormer ships a frozen ESM2 protein embedding and never updates it; scPRINT started from a protein-derived table and trained it; scGPT and AIDO.Cell learned theirs from expression alone. So we built a gene table directly from protein sequence with two public ESM2 checkpoints and ran it through the identical translator, folds and scoring. It reaches 0.2331 (0.1035 to 0.4356) in HepG2 and 0.2642 (0.1959 to 0.3269) in Jurkat, beating co-expression on the same held-out perturbations by 0.1525 (0.06469 to 0.2901) and 0.1564 (0.1048 to 0.2026). Paired against the single-cell models' own tables it is a tie in seven of the eight comparisons and ahead of scGPT in Jurkat. Protein sequence alone therefore supplies a gene table that does this job as well as the single-cell models' tables do. That says where the signal can come from; it does not say where scGPT's comes from, since scGPT's table carries no protein-space structure and still scores as well.

What carries that signal is the model's static gene embedding, not its simulated reaction to a perturbation. Adding the response features to the embedding changes the score by less than the design can resolve, and on their own the response features are weak everywhere. How weak depends on the score: on the fold-centred one they stay inside the shuffled-label band in HepG2 and clear it slightly for two of three models in Jurkat, while on the pre-registered score TranscriptFormer's response features score above co-expression in HepG2 (0.06533 against 0.04879), by less than the paired comparison separates. Both readings are reported throughout.

Three limits change how far this reads. The pre-registered score rewards an arm for the part of a held-out group's response that its members share, which a prediction that barely varies with the perturbation can also get; a fold-centred version that strips that shared part is reported beside it everywhere, but it removes the held-out fold's own mean and so is a diagnostic rather than a prediction a model could make in advance. The absolute numbers are small: the score is already divided by the reproducibility limit, so 0.27 means the arm recovers about a quarter of the reproducible perturbation-specific signal on a correlation scale. And the second pre-registered axis, perturbation discrimination, depends on which release of the metric computes it, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the ranking.

Two scores: the pre-registered rule, and a fold-centred diagnostic beside it

The verdict is read from the rule fixed before the run: the reliability-weighted correlation between the predicted and the measured residual response, corrected for the reproducibility ceiling. Residual here means against the mean effect of the TRAINING folds, so nothing about a held-out perturbation enters its own score.

That rule has a known soft spot, and it is worth stating plainly because it shapes how the numbers should be read. Perturbations inside one held-out group share a per-gene response, so a prediction that barely varies with the perturbation still correlates with the measurement. Permuting the perturbation labels inside the training fold and refitting reaches 0.04123 in HepG2 and 0.0444 in Jurkat with the ridge translator, as large as the co-expression arm's own pre-registered score (0.04879 and 0.04462); with the network translator the draws reach 0.02764 and 0.02351. Those are the ceilings a score has to clear, and the embedding arms sit five to ten times above them.

Removing each read-out gene's mean over the held-out fold from both the prediction and the measurement takes the shared component out. On that fold-centred score the same ridge draws fall to 0.01143 and 0.01782. Every arm is reported on both scores throughout. The fold-centred one is a diagnostic, not a second verdict: it subtracts a mean of the held-out outcomes, which no model could compute in advance, and it was written after seeing the shuffled draws, so it is post hoc. What it answers is narrower than the pre-registered question: not whether an arm predicts the response, but whether it tells one held-out perturbation apart from another.

The two scores rank the arms similarly but not identically (rank correlation 0.8523 in HepG2 and 0.8679 in Jurkat over every arm), and what moves between them is the set of weak arms. The embedding arms are far ahead on both. Among the weak arms the order changes: on the pre-registered score TranscriptFormer's response features reach 0.06533 in HepG2 against co-expression's 0.04879, but the paired difference on the same held-out perturbations is 0.01369 (-0.01946 to 0.03119), inside the 0.04002 this design resolves once the response groups are resampled; on the fold-centred score the same pair is a tie as well. Where the two scores disagree, this page says so rather than picking the flattering one.

Model embeddings clear the null; co-expression does not

11 of the 12 foundation-model arms clear the shuffled-label null in HepG2 and 11 of 12 in Jurkat; the one that fails is scGPT's response-only arm, in both lines. Co-expression barely clears it: 0.04879 (0.02346 to 0.09292) in HepG2 against a null ceiling of 0.02764, and 0.04462 (0.0302 to 0.0617) in Jurkat against 0.02351. Every one of the embedding arms beats it on the same held-out perturbations, paired: 3 of 3 in HepG2 and 3 of 3 in Jurkat have a paired interval clear of zero. The largest is 0.1846 (0.1501 to 0.2408) for scGPT embedding in HepG2 and 0.1371 (0.09442 to 0.1819) for TranscriptFormer embedding + response in Jurkat, against a smallest difference this design resolves of 0.05942 and 0.05874. The paired difference is taken on the per-perturbation correlations before the ceiling correction, so it is smaller than the gap between the two corrected scores above.

Ridge regression on the same features scores near zero for almost every arm, so whatever the network finds is not linear in the concatenated pair feature.

Throughout this section 'foundation-model arm' means an arm built on one of the four single-cell models, which is how they are packaged, not a claim about where the feature came from. Two of the four gene tables trace back to a protein language model rather than to single-cell data; a later section reads that out of the checkpoints and runs a raw protein table through the same pipeline.

The signal is in the gene embedding, not the response features

The previous run harvested response features by querying each model with a gene set to a different expression level and recording how the read-out genes moved. Those features are eleven numbers per pair. Added to the embedding they change the score by at most 0.0201, with no consistent sign, and adding co-expression to the embedding changes it by at most 0.02954, also with no consistent sign. Neither addition reaches the smallest difference this design can resolve. What predicts the response is the static gene embedding.

On their own the response features are weak, and how weak depends on which score is read. Their largest fold-centred score anywhere is 0.03438. In HepG2 none of the three models clears the shuffled-label ceiling of 0.0286. In Jurkat scPRINT and TranscriptFormer clear the lower ceiling there (0.0204), the largest reaching 0.03438, above the null and still between a third and a fifth of the embedding arms in the same cell line. On the pre-registered score 3 of the six model-and-line combinations put the response features numerically ahead of co-expression, none of them by more than the paired difference resolves. The claim these numbers support is that response features add nothing to the embedding and do not overturn co-expression as a class, not that they carry no signal at all.

Every arm above that beat co-expression fed the translator a model's static gene-identity table. Two of those four tables did not come from single-cell data alone, so what the phrase 'foundation-model embedding' covers has to be split apart. The two tables agree weakly, and the hybrid test was too underpowered to say whether that buys anything

The result above leaves one thing open. scGPT's table scores as well as the protein tables while the similarity measure says it carries no protein-space structure at all, so either the two tables are different routes to the same underlying quantity or they hold different information. Concatenating them answers it directly: the translator gets scGPT's 512-wide vector and the raw ESM2-3B 2,560-wide vector for each of the two genes, 6,144 columns in total, with the same network shape, optimizer, folds, weighting, ceiling correction and scoring as every other arm. The arm is wider than either part, which is where a dimensionality artefact would show up, so a fixed random vector per gene at exactly 6,144 columns is scored beside it.

The reading was fixed before the arm was scored. If the concatenation does not beat the better of its two parts by more than the paired comparison resolves, and the two parts succeed and fail on the same held-out perturbations, they are proxies for one quantity reached by different routes. If it clearly beats both and they succeed on different perturbations, they carry different information, which is a separate finding rather than a footnote to this one. Agreement is the Spearman correlation of the two arms' per-perturbation scores: 0.5 or above is the same perturbations, below 0.3 is different ones, and anything between is neither.

The concatenation does not beat the better of its two parts, and the comparison could not have resolved a gain of the size on offer either: in all four cells the difference against the better part is smaller than the smallest difference the paired comparison resolves - HepG2 grouped +0.02233 against 0.1366; HepG2 random +0.02134 against 0.04688; Jurkat grouped +0.004363 against 0.02620; Jurkat random +0.01529 against 0.03364, the better part being scGPT embedding in HepG2 grouped and ESM2-3B protein embedding in the other three. That is a property of this design rather than of the models, so the arm cannot be read as the two tables being equivalent or either one being redundant. What it does rule out is the extra width: the two-table arm sits far above its own width-matched random projection, so the added columns are not what is being measured. The fold-centred diagnostic agrees on 3 of the 4 comparisons against the better part; in Jurkat under the grouped split against ESM2-3B protein embedding (+0.0234, 0.001476 to 0.03371, against 0.02324 resolvable) it clears what it resolves, by a margin narrower than the interval is wide.

The half of the rule that does carry information is the agreement statistic, and it only means something beside its calibration. The scGPT-table arm and the protein-table arm agree on which held-out perturbations they score well at Spearman 0.2503 in HepG2 and 0.2394 in Jurkat. Two sizes of the same protein model - two views of one underlying quantity in this exact setup - agree at 0.6816 in HepG2 and 0.6727 in Jurkat. scGPT against co-expression agrees at 0.2843 in HepG2 and 0.2295 in Jurkat, and scGPT against TranscriptFormer at 0.2708 in HepG2 and 0.2651 in Jurkat. So scGPT's table agrees with a protein model about as weakly as it agrees with co-expression, and far below what the same model at two sizes reaches - below the 0.3 the rule set for different perturbations, and nowhere near the 0.5 it set for the same ones. Per-perturbation scores are noisy at this sample size, which is why the same-family pair is the reference that matters: it shows what this statistic reaches when two arms really are two routes to one quantity.

Read against the rule, that is mixed: anything else, reported as mixed rather than resolved either way. By cell and split, HepG2 under the grouped split reads mixed, HepG2 under the random split reads mixed, Jurkat under the grouped split reads mixed and Jurkat under the random split reads mixed. The finding is a discrepancy identified rather than resolved. The weak agreement points toward scGPT's table and the protein table carrying different information rather than being redundant routes to one quantity, and the concatenation test is too underpowered to say whether that difference converts into a gain this translator can use. Neither half of that is a null and neither is a positive. It does not say scGPT's table is protein-derived either; the checkpoint audit already says it is not, and the similarity measure still finds no protein geometry in it. A task with finer resolution than a per-perturbation correlation, or a different way of combining the two tables, is what would settle it.

Discrimination, the second scoring axis, depends on the version that computes it

Correlation is not the only way to score this. Perturbation discrimination asks whether a predicted profile is closer to its own perturbation's measured profile than to the other held-out ones, which is this run's question with no residualization in it anywhere. It is computed with Arc Institute's own cell-eval rather than a reimplementation, on effect-scale profiles reconstituted by adding the training fold's mean effect back to the predicted residual. Chance is 0.5.

Under the version the jobs resolved, the embedding arms clear chance in both lines (0.5309 in HepG2, 0.5793 in Jurkat), co-expression does not (0.4647 and 0.4845), and neither do the response-only arms. That ordering is the one both correlation scores give, so it would be independent support for the result, except that the ordering is not stable across releases of the metric.

The pre-registration pinned cell-eval==0.5.19. That version does not exist: the package publishes 0.1.0 and then 0.5.33 onward, and the upstream tag list jumps 0.5.18 to 0.5.21, so the pin was unattainable when it was written and the jobs resolved 0.8.2. Rescoring the same saved prediction matrices under 0.5.18, 0.5.21 and 0.8.2 reverses the comparison this axis is being asked about. Under 0.5.18, which is identical to 0.5.21 on every file, the HepG2 scGPT full arm scores 0.4692 against 0.5135 for co-expression and the Jurkat TranscriptFormer full arm 0.4632 against 0.4986; under 0.8.2 the same matrices give 0.5377 against 0.4647 and 0.5773 against 0.4845. The largest move on any file is 0.1141. The implementations differ in one line (0.5.x scores the absolute effect profile, 0.8.2 the signed one) and a distance on absolute values discards the direction of every response, which is why 0.8.2 is the reported number and the version travels with it.

Because the two existing versions nearest the unattainable pin rank co-expression and the model arms the other way round, this axis cannot serve as independent confirmation of the correlation result, and it is reported as inconclusive rather than as corroboration. It is not evidence against the correlation result either: the reversal is a property of the metric implementation, not of the predictions, which are the same matrices in both cases.

No query anchor lifts the response features out of the null band

The paper's mechanism claim is that querying the model at a uniform virtual value, rather than at the expression the cell actually has, gives a cleaner read of one gene's influence on another. The features were harvested at four queries in the previous run, and this step scores the same response-only arm at each of them in HepG2 under the grouped split. Translator, split, scope and scoring rule are identical, so the only thing that varies is what the model was asked.

One query needs care. At the individual-cells anchor the response is averaged over 4 real control cells, and a gene that already reads zero in one of them is unchanged by being set to 0 CP10K: that cell's two forward passes are the same call, so it contributes an exact zero. Those no-op queries are excluded per (perturbed gene, value) rather than by deleting the whole dose, because the same dose is a real measurement for every other perturbation. Since the stored row is the plain mean over the cells and the feature is that row z-scored across read-out genes, excluding them is a positive scale factor the z-score removes exactly, which is checked against the shipped arrays: 584 no-op queries over the three blocks touch 431 query rows of 7860, the corrected feature differs from the shipped one by at most 1.9e-06, and 15 of 15 checks pass. 2 rows were no-ops in every cell and carry no measurement at all. The masked column is narrower for an unrelated reason: that variant ships no gradient form.

The anchor moves the score, but never out of the null band. The largest paired move is -0.01195 (-0.01823 to -0.002416) (TranscriptFormer at the uniform virtual anchor). The uniform-virtual anchor beats the on-distribution one for scGPT and loses for TranscriptFormer, so the mechanism claim holds for some models and reverses for others on this task. The best score at any anchor is 0.02076, still below the shuffled-label ceiling of 0.0286 and a fraction of the 0.1089 the same models' embeddings reach in this cell line. Masking is not a clean mechanistic contrast: it hides the read-out gene's own value together with its whole block, so it removes context as well as the value.

The mean-control arms were re-run inside this job as its own reference. They reproduce the main sweep exactly, to every digit stored, which also confirms the rebuilt bundle is the same bundle.

A dropped arm: the source paper's BEELINE benchmark did not reproduce

One side arm was run and then dropped, and it is written up here so that the decision is on the record. The response features this run tests come from a paper whose own evidence is a different task: BEELINE network inference, where a projector maps a gene-pair feature to a regulatory edge and is scored by AUPRC on held-out datasets. Disagreeing with its conclusion is worth more after reproducing its setting, so its scGPT embedding baseline was re-run on its own benchmark files: the projector fitted on the STRING/hESC pair set (8,065 pairs, balanced 1:1) and scored on the held-out cells with the paper's projector shape, optimizer, batch size and epoch count.

It did not reproduce. Mean AUPRC over the three held-out cells is 0.6724 against 0.6247 reported, a mean difference of +0.0477 and up to 0.09176 on one cell, higher on two of the three cells and lower on the third. No tolerance was pre-registered for this side result and no seed-to-seed noise estimate was run, so the gap is unexplained and the re-implementation is not established as the paper's setting.

So the arm is dropped rather than reported. A check that disagrees with the source it is checking is not a check, and reporting anything measured inside it would invite the reading that the paper was reproduced and then refuted, which is not what happened. Controls were run in that setting and are not reported as findings here, because a control is only informative once the setting it runs in is established. The gradient-trajectory and virtual-value arms were not re-run at all: they would need the feature extraction repeated against these datasets, which is a separate harvest. Nothing else on this page depends on this arm - the translator scores, the protein control and the cross-line transfer result are all measured on the CRISPRi screens and never touch the BEELINE data - and the full output stays in the artifact record so the attempt is auditable.

Controls: node statistics, split design, and shared control cells

A pair feature can score without saying anything about the pair. Two controls bound that. The node-only control gives the network the same six summary statistics for each gene on its own (expression level, detection rate, co-expression degree) and no pairwise term. Fitted as a free network over both genes it reaches 0.1154 in HepG2 and 0.0477 in Jurkat on the pre-registered rule, well below scGPT embedding at 0.2719 but above co-expression, a reminder that part of what any arm scores here is what the two genes are like on their own. Restricted to an additive form, one branch per gene summed, it is identically zero on the fold-centred score: after the fold's per-gene mean is removed, an additive prediction is the same vector for every perturbation. That is a property of the control and the score together, not a measurement, which is why the free version is the informative one.

The gap between the two splits is the second bound. Under a random split, where correlated perturbations can fall on both sides, node statistics alone reach 0.19 in HepG2 on the pre-registered rule against 0.1154 under the grouped split, and on the fold-centred score the same control goes from 0.03656 to 0.2022, 5.529 times its grouped-split score. A control with no pairwise term should not gain from where the fold boundary falls, and that it does is what the grouped split is there to prevent.

Transfer across cell lines survives only against a gene-identity control

Training the translator on one screen and scoring it on the other looks like strong transfer until the gene-identity control is read. Over every gene shared by the two screens the best model arm reaches 0.277, and a fixed random vector per gene, a feature that carries nothing but which gene this is, reaches 0.2441. The two screens perturb mostly the same genes, and their responses to the same knockdown are correlated, so gene identity alone nearly solves that evaluation.

Restricting the held-out side to perturbations whose gene was never perturbed in the training screen removes it. The gene-identity null falls to 0.02728 or below, and the model arms keep 0.1262 at best. Co-expression does not transfer in either direction. The unseen-gene scope holds 89 and 78 held-out perturbations, so the intervals are wide.

That surviving score is where the four gene tables come apart. The protein section below reads the same unseen-gene evaluation arm by arm: on the rows this table reports, the tables that derive from protein sequence keep an interval clear of zero and scGPT's, the one learned from expression alone, does not in either direction.

The destination line's response has to be measured against something, and the choice matters more than it looks. Using the source line's mean, or all destination cells, instead of a held-out half of the destination cells moves an arm's pre-registered score by up to 0.5616, and the ordering it induces agrees with the held-out-reference one only loosely (rank correlation as low as 0.4354 within a direction and scope). The two alternatives also score twice as many perturbations, because the held-out reference has to be built out of half of them. Every number reported here uses the held-out reference cells, the only one of the three that keeps the evaluation non-transductive, and counts from the three are never pooled.

The ranking holds under every scoring choice except the raw target

The score has three choices in it: which target (the residual response, the fold-centred response, or the raw log2 fold change), whether to weight read-out genes by their reliability, and whether to divide by the reproducibility ceiling. All twelve combinations are computed for every arm. Weighting and the ceiling correction change nothing about the ordering, and the residual and fold-centred targets rank the arms similarly (rank correlation at least 0.8247 against the pre-registered cell). The arms that move between those two are the weak ones, the response-only arms in particular; the embedding arms are ahead under both.

The raw target is the exception: it ranks the arms differently (rank correlation -0.1319). Predicting the raw fold change is mostly predicting each read-out gene's average response over all perturbations, which every arm can do and which says nothing about the perturbation. That is the component the pre-registered target removes and the fold-centred target removes again on the held-out side.

Limitations

  • The pre-registered score rewards predicting what a held-out group shares. Its shuffled-label draws sit above zero for that reason, and a prediction that barely varies with the perturbation collects part of the score. The fold-centred version reported beside it removes that component, but it does so with a mean of the held-out outcomes, so it is a diagnostic rather than a second verdict, and it was written after the shuffled draws were seen. Where the two disagree this page says so; neither is presented as confirming the other.
  • The effect is small in absolute terms. Every score is already divided by the square root of the reproducibility ceiling (0.6847 in HepG2), so 1.0 would mean predicting the response as well as the measurement repeats itself. scGPT embedding reaches 0.2719 there, which leaves most of the reproducible perturbation-specific signal unexplained by every arm tested.
  • The independent unit is the response group, not the perturbation. HepG2's 524 in-scope perturbations fall into 226 groups of correlated responses, one holding 149 of them, and Jurkat's into 452. Every interval on this page resamples those groups, which is wider than resampling rows and is why some differences that would read as separated do not. The count of effectively independent units is closer to the group count than to the perturbation count.
  • One translator, and four anchors in one cell line. Every arm went through the same network shape; a larger translator could change the absolute numbers, though it would have to change them unevenly across arms to change the ordering. The anchor was varied in HepG2 only, over the three models with full-coverage feature blocks; AIDO.Cell's block covers 250 perturbations, so it cannot enter the full-scope sensitivity step at all.
  • The discrimination axis is inconclusive. The version pinned before the run does not exist upstream, and the two real versions nearest it rank co-expression above the model arms while the current one ranks them below, on the same saved predictions. The reported numbers name their version, and the verdict does not rest on them.
  • The source paper's own benchmark is not part of the evidence here. A re-implementation of it was run and dropped: its projector and embedding baseline scored 0.6724 against 0.6247 reported, up to 0.09176 apart on one cell, with no pre-registered tolerance and no noise estimate, so the setting is not established and no result on this page rests on it or on any control run inside it. Its virtual-value and gradient-trajectory features were never re-run, because those would need a fresh extraction against those datasets.
  • Two cell lines from one screen. Both come from the same CRISPRi Perturb-seq study with the same protocol, so the cross-line result is a weaker test of generality than two independent screens would be.
  • Co-expression is one estimator family. Four statistics over one half of the control cells stand in for the statistical baseline; a network-inference method fitted to the same cells could do better than they do.
  • The two-table test is one concatenation at one width. It asks whether this translator can use scGPT's table and the protein table together better than it uses the better one alone, and it does not rule out that a different way of combining them, or a task with finer resolution than a per-perturbation correlation, would separate what they carry.
  • The protein control shows where the signal can come from, not where each model's signal does come from. It establishes that a gene table built from protein sequence alone scores as well as the single-cell models' tables on this task, and the checkpoint audit establishes which tables are protein-derived. It does not decompose scGPT's or AIDO.Cell's learned table into a protein-like part and a cell-specific part, and it says nothing about what these models do beyond this gene-pair task. One protein model at two sizes, and one pooling rule (mean over the final layer), stand in for protein space.
